## Page 001 - Parties, Purpose and Contract Structure

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 1.1 Parties

This Operations and Maintenance Agreement is made between Westmere Industrial Park Authority (Owner) and Sunhaven Energy Operations Ltd. (Operator), effective 10 March 2027. The Owner has installed a distributed generation system to support industrial tenants in the fictional North Basin Industrial Park, Nevada.

### 1.2 Purpose

Operator will perform preventive maintenance, corrective maintenance, remote monitoring and performance reporting for the solar and battery assets listed in Appendix A. Operator may not represent itself as the electricity retailer and must follow the Owner's dispatch protocol.

### 1.3 Order of Precedence

This agreement governs service standards; Appendix A identifies covered assets; Appendix B defines the safety plan; Appendix C sets reporting templates. A signed amendment controls over inconsistent provisions only to the extent expressly stated.

## Page 002 - Asset Register and Operating Boundaries

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 2.1 Asset Scope

The covered portfolio consists of a 7.5 MWdc solar photovoltaic array, a 4 MW / 16 MWh lithium iron phosphate battery system, six medium-voltage transformers and the site supervisory control system. The assets are located at two fenced operating yards.

### 2.2 Exclusions

Operator is not responsible for the public utility's off-site feeder, Owner-owned tenant building circuits, or third-party telecommunications beyond the site demarcation. Excluded systems may still require incident coordination when they affect safe operation.

### 2.3 Asset Records

Within 30 days of commencement, Operator will reconcile serial numbers, warranties and key commissioning records against Owner's register. Discrepancies exceeding 2 percent of listed critical components must be escalated with a corrective inventory plan.

## Page 003 - Service Term and Mobilization

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 3.1 Term

The service term begins on 1 May 2027 and continues for five years through 30 April 2032. The Parties may extend the service for one further two-year period by mutual written agreement signed no later than 90 days before expiration.

### 3.2 Mobilization

Operator must complete safety induction, spare-parts inspection and remote monitoring handover during the first 20 Business Days. Owner will provide complete access credentials and available manufacturer manuals before the first scheduled maintenance event.

### 3.3 Transition

A joint handover report will list outstanding warranty claims, open alerts and deferred work orders. Signing the handover report does not waive pre-existing defects attributable to EPC construction or Owner direction.

## Page 004 - Service Fee, Variable Costs and Invoicing

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 4.1 Fixed Fee

Owner will pay Operator a fixed service fee of USD 42,000 per calendar month, inclusive of routine inspections, remote monitoring and ordinary preventive maintenance labor.

### 4.2 Parts and Emergencies

Replacement parts with a unit purchase cost exceeding USD 2,500 require Owner's written approval unless immediate replacement is necessary to prevent serious safety harm. Approved emergency call-out labor outside normal hours is billed at USD 145 per technician-hour.

### 4.3 Invoicing

Operator invoices monthly in arrears, with payment due within 25 calendar days of invoice receipt. Undisputed late balances accrue interest at 0.75 percent per month, subject to the maximum permitted by law.

## Page 005 - Preventive Maintenance and Response Times

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 5.1 Preventive Plan

Operator will inspect inverter stations monthly, conduct infrared thermography quarterly, test protective relays annually and inspect battery fire-suppression controls every six months. Maintenance tasks must be recorded against asset identifiers.

### 5.2 Incident Response

A Critical Incident involving active fire, imminent electrical hazard or complete microgrid shutdown requires remote acknowledgement within 10 minutes and qualified on-site attendance within two hours. A High Incident affecting more than 20 percent of available generation capacity requires acknowledgement within 30 minutes.

### 5.3 Deferral

Maintenance may be deferred only when weather, a permit condition or safety requires it. The deferral record must state justification, protective controls and the revised target date approved by Owner.

## Page 006 - Performance Guarantees and Adjustments

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 6.1 Solar Availability

Operator guarantees an annual technical availability of 98.5 percent for the photovoltaic array, measured excluding grid outages, approved maintenance and force majeure events.

### 6.2 Battery Throughput

The Operator must use dispatch instructions and battery state-of-health safeguards; it does not guarantee a fixed battery energy throughput because Owner controls dispatch.

### 6.3 Fee Adjustment

If annual PV technical availability falls below 98.5 percent for causes within Operator control, the annual service fee is reduced by 1 percent for each 0.5 percentage-point shortfall, capped at 6 percent of the annual fixed fee. This reduction is the sole contractual price remedy for that availability shortfall.

## Page 007 - Safety, Environmental Controls and Reporting

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 7.1 Safety Protocol

All energized electrical work requires a documented permit-to-work, lockout-tagout and arc-flash controls. Operator will maintain a site-specific emergency response plan and conduct two emergency drills every contract year.

### 7.2 Environmental Requirements

Operator will manage damaged battery modules as regulated waste and use licensed transporters where required. Any confirmed electrolyte release reaching soil must be reported to Owner within one hour of discovery.

### 7.3 Reporting

Operator must deliver a monthly performance report by the fifth Business Day of the following month, with uptime, maintenance completion, incident log, energy production and open defects. Supporting raw telemetry will be retained for 24 months.

## Page 008 - Spare Parts, Subcontractors and Cybersecurity

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 8.1 Critical Spares

Operator must maintain at least four inverter cooling fan kits, eight DC string fuses and two fire-panel sensor assemblies at the on-site store. Parts drawn down must be replenished within 15 Business Days if commercially available.

### 8.2 Subcontracting

Specialist high-voltage services may be subcontracted with Owner's prior written approval. Operator remains responsible for subcontractor safety, supervision and performance as if it performed the work itself.

### 8.3 Cybersecurity

Operator will use individual named accounts, multifactor authentication and encrypted remote access for supervisory control. A confirmed compromise of operational control credentials must be notified to Owner within two hours of confirmation.

## Page 009 - Insurance, Liability and Termination

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 9.1 Coverage

Operator shall maintain employer liability insurance as required by law, commercial general liability insurance of USD 3 million per occurrence and professional liability insurance of USD 2 million per claim.

### 9.2 Cap

Except for fraud, intentional wrongdoing and bodily injury caused by negligence, Operator's aggregate contractual liability is limited to 100 percent of the fixed service fees paid or payable in the preceding twelve months.

### 9.3 Default

A material nonmonetary breach may be cured within 30 calendar days after written notice; a repeated Critical Incident response failure on three separate occasions within any rolling twelve months permits termination after an opportunity to respond in writing.

## Page 010 - Exit Assistance, Notices and Disputes

<!-- PAGE_BREAK -->

### 10.1 Exit Support

During the final 45 days of the term, Operator will provide a live defect register, updated asset inventory, inspection history and operating credentials to Owner's designated replacement operator. Exit support exceeding 40 labor-hours is billed at USD 95 per hour with prior written approval.

### 10.2 Notice

Formal notices must be delivered by courier or registered mail to the addresses specified in the executed signature pages, with an optional email duplicate. A message sent only to the operations helpdesk is not a formal notice.

### 10.3 Law and Mediation

Nevada law governs this agreement. Before filing an ordinary contractual lawsuit, senior representatives must attempt good-faith mediation in Reno, Nevada, for at least 20 Business Days after one Party submits a written mediation request.
